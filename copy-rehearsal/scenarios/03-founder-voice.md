# Scenario 03: founder-supplied copy

Use this when the founder already has copy and asks Lifty to improve it while
preserving their voice. Keep this input fixed after review so later drafts can
be compared with the baseline.

## Rehearsal request

The founder wants Lifty to improve the copy below. Keep their voice, facts, and
call to action. Do not replace it with a generic LIFT-sounding sequence. Do not
activate or send anything.

## Saved company (fictional)

- Company: Nettle & Co.
- Company type: early-stage professional services, about $1.2M ARR, 6 people
- Why it might use Lifty: Reese is the founder-seller and already does
  outbound to studio founders. They want Lifty to clean up existing copy.
- Website: `https://www.nettleandco.example` (founder-confirmed)
- Founder / sender: Reese Lang, founder
- One-line description: Nettle & Co. does fractional bookkeeping for design
  studios so the founder is not closing the books on Sunday night.
- Offer: a monthly close plus a short Monday numbers note for studios of 8-40
  people.
- Audience: founders and studio managers at independent design studios in the
  US and Canada.
- Disqualifiers: public accounting firms, venture-backed SaaS, and companies
  with a full-time controller.
- Commercial voice: dry, slightly wry, first-person plural. Reese avoids
  exclamation points, "unlock," "scale," and demo asks. They would rather get a
  yes/no reply than a booked meeting.
- Preferred CTA: "If the books still live in a founder's Sunday night, reply yes."
- Supporting evidence the founder supplied:
  - They currently close the books for 11 design studios.
  - One studio founder told them, "I stopped opening QuickBooks on weekends."
  - They do not claim revenue lift or time-saved percentages.

## Sample lead (for audience context only)

The reviewed sample includes Alex Kim, founder of Harbor Type Studio, 14 people.
Templates must still work for other current and future eligible A/B leads. Do
not write copy that only makes sense for Alex or Harbor Type Studio.

## Founder-supplied copy to improve

### LinkedIn

Invitation: none (keep it that way).

Message 1 after acceptance:

> I hope this finds you well. We are Nettle & Co. We provide fractional
> bookkeeping, monthly closes, cash-flow visibility, QuickBooks cleanup, and
> a dedicated bookkeeping partner for growing studios. I'd love to unlock a
> seamless finance function for your team. Would you be open to a quick demo
> this week?

Message 2:

> Just checking in on my last note. Curious if you had a chance to review.

Message 3:

> Circling back one more time. We help studios scale their back office. Happy
> to show you a demo if useful.

### Email

1. Subject: Quick intro  
   Body: Hi {{first_name}}, I hope this email finds you well. Nettle & Co. is a
   fractional bookkeeping partner. We unlock seamless monthly closes for design
   studios. Open to a 15-minute demo?

2. Subject: Following up  
   Body: Just circling back in case this got buried.

3. Subject: Checking in  
   Body: Hi {{first_name}}, we provide fractional bookkeeping, monthly closes,
   cash-flow visibility, QuickBooks cleanup, and a dedicated partner. Would you
   be open to a demo?

4. Subject: One more thought  
   Body: Wanted to reach out again about unlocking your finance function.

5. Subject: Final attempt  
   Body: I'll close the loop here. If you ever want a demo, let me know.

## What to preserve

Reese's dry tone, the Sunday-night books observation, the 11-studio fact if it
stays honest as a company fact, the customer quote if it is clearly a quote,
and the yes/no CTA. Do not add ROI numbers, extra services, or a meeting-first
ask unless Reese already used one.
